## Midterm

We are halfway through our module on understanding Buddhism through its Scriptures and I want to pause to give you an opportunity to synthesize your learning. You will do this in an essay that uses some of the interpretive tools and perspectives of this class to interpret a passage from the autobiography of one of the most admired modern Thai monks, Phra Ajaan Lee.

One issue we have been stressing in the course is that religions are internally diverse and that by looking at how Buddhists have approached their scriptures, we can see that diversity is part of the scriptures themselves.

Please use the paragraphs below as the basis of an essay in which you persuade someone else (perhaps in this class) that the described incident teaches us something very important about how Buddhists see their scriptures and as a result, it teaches us something very important about the internal diversity of Buddhist scriptures and the Buddhist world itself.

Try to make use of the details in the passage. Phra Ajaan Lee mentions lots of details and you can ask yourself if they become important—if they add significance and density--when we remember something about other Buddhist scriptures or about Buddhist scriptures in general. For example, when Phra Ajaan Lee says, "One day, for instance, at about five in the evening," does it become more significant when we recall the fact that Buddhist scriptures are things taught skillfully for particular persons at particular times?

In the passage, I have put some explanatory comments in brackets { . . } to facilitate ease of reading:

---

>When Ajaan Mun and Phra Paññabhisara Thera {his monastic teachers; Ajaan is a title for a teacher} returned to Bangkok to spend the Rains Retreat {a time of seclusion when monks devote themselves to religious activities} at Wat Sra Pathum {name of a Buddhist temple; you can find an account of it here, with pictures: http://www.sacredsiam.com/thailand-temples/wat-pathum-wanaram-bangkok.html}, they left me under the guidance of Ajaan Singh and Ajaan MahaPin {two other monastic teachers}. During this period I followed Ajaan Singh and Ajaan MahaPin on their wanderings through the countryside. They had been asked by Phraya Trang, The Prince of Ubon {Ubon is a city in northeast Thailand, a part of the country reputed for the practices of forest monks devoted to religious practice}, to teach morality and meditation to the people of the rural areas. When the time came to enter the Rains Retreat, we stopped at OxHead Village Monastery in Yasothon district. It so happened that Somdet Phra Mahawirawong , the ecclesiastical head of the Northeast {Somdet and Phra are monastic titles, given in recognition of a monk's spiritual achievements}, called Ajaan MahaPin back to the city of Ubon, so in the end only six of us spent the rainy season together in that township.
>
>I was very ardent in my efforts to practice meditation that rainy season, but there were times I couldn't help feeling a little discouraged because all my teachers had left me. Occasionally I'd think of disrobing {i.e. leaving the monastic order}, but whenever I felt this way there'd always be something to bring me back to my senses.
>
>One day, for instance, at about five in the evening, I was doing walking meditation but my thoughts had strayed towards worldly matters. A woman happened to walk past the monastery, improvising a song—'I've seen the heart of the tyd tyy bird: Its mouth is singing, tyd tyy, tyd tyy, but its heart is out looking for crabs'—so I memorized her song and repeated it over and over, telling myself, "It's you she's singing about. Here you are, a monk, trying to develop some virtue inside yourself, and yet you let your heart go looking for worldly matters.' I felt ashamed of myself. I decided that I'd have to bring my heart in line with the fact that I was a monk if I didn't want the woman's song to apply to me. The whole incident thus turned into Dhamma.

From the Autobiography of Phra Ajaan Lee, pp 9-10 http://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/thai/lee/leeauto.pdf

For the purposes of this essay, you should not use anything else from Phra Ajaan Lee's Autobiography, but those of you who want to read more about a lived Buddhist life will find much to engage you in it.

Your essay should have a theme or argument and it should persuade.

In terms of developing a theme for your essay, you might first focus on something like one of these sentences from the passage:

"The whole incident thus turned into Dhamma." {How should we understand the word Dhamma (Dharma) here?}

"It's you she's singing about." {remember the Buddhist idea that texts become scripture (sutra) when their threads (sutra) are woven into a fabric with threads from a particular human life.}

"whenever I felt this way there'd always be something to bring me back to my senses." {are scriptures something that can bring us "back to our senses"? What else can they do?}

Or you might develop a theme around a question, like: when does a scripture begin (when it is said {is the woman's song itself a scripture?} or when it is heard, or when it is practiced?); or what is the significance of the parable-like nature of a scripture in this

Or you might develop a theme around a comparison, like the Buddha teaching with a flower or Ananda's sense of loss anticipating being without a teacher as in the Mahaparinibbanasutta.

There are lots of ways to develop themes, these are just two, but just be sure that your essay has a theme or argument.

In your essay, try also to use at least two of the following interpretive tools that we have considered so far in the course.

- Open/closed canon
- Dharma as what the Buddha Taught/Dharma as
- Dharma to be studied, Dharma to be practiced, Dharma to be realized
- Existential concern and scripture
- Being scriptured

Essays should be no more than 500 words long. Please post your essay and respond to at least two other posts from your fellow students.

Your responses to your peers should answer the following questions:

- What aspects of this response stood out to you? What persuaded you the most?
- Did it make good use of the details of the passage? Did it illuminate the passage by effective use of two listed interpretive tools or effective use of inference?
- What else did the essay do well? How could it be improved?
