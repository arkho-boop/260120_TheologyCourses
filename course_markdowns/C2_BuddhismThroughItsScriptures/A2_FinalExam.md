## Final Exam
The final assessment essay is an opportunity to synthesize your learning in this module. Choose one of the following three options according to which you think will best help you to synthesize your learning in this module.

### Option One: Use What Was Learned to Inform Others

Look at the Visual Parable of the Monkey Reaching for the Moon and then give an interpretation of its components [full moon; buckets; moon reflections; clouds; flower petals; monkey's actions; "Zen circle" (click [here](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Enso.jpg); if you want to see a Zen circle painted by Shibayama Zenkei, one can be found [here](http://madinkbeard.com/archives/enso-and-haiga)); etc] as illustrating Buddhist perspectives on scriptures.

You should direct your exposition to someone who knows nothing about Buddhism.

### Option Two: Engage in Critical Self-Reflection

How we approach any area of inquiry always conditions and shapes what we are able to see. Our approach always reveals certain things even as it always conceals certain things.

What does an approach to Buddhism through its scriptures reveal about Buddhism as a religious heritage, and what does the same approach also conceal?

If we approached the study of Buddhism as a religious heritage by beginning with some core doctrines (for example, "Four Noble Truths," "the Interconnectedness of All Things," "Compassion," etc) what would have been missed about Buddhism that is displayed by approaching Buddhism through its scriptures?

### Option Three: Extend What Has Been Learned To A New Area

Throughout the class, we have seen how Buddhists have used some scriptures to interpret other scriptures, as one of the ways that they handled the diversity of scriptures within the Buddhist heritage.

We have also seen, in Day 7, how some modern Zen Masters and the Dalai Lama have used Buddhist scriptures to interpret non-Buddhist Scriptures.

Using the approach of the Dalai Lama in which he attended to how something in the Gospel of Mark resonated with something from what he knew of Buddhist scriptures, choose a piece of scripture from any religious tradition and see what kind of interpretation emerges for you if you allow it to resonate with something that you have learned about Buddhist scriptures in this module.

Essays should be no more than 500 words long. Please post your essay and respond to at least two other posts from your fellow students.

Your responses to your peers should answer the following questions:

- What aspects of this response stood out to you? What persuaded you the most?
- Did it make good use of the details of the passage? Did it illuminate the passage by effective use of two listed interpretive tools or effective use of inference?
- What else did the essay do well? How could it be improved?
